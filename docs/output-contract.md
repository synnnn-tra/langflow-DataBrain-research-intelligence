# Research brief output contract

DBRI's target output is a readable and evidence-aware research brief. The sections below are the recommended output contract for evaluation; they are not a claim that every section is already enforced by the current formatter.

## Required sections

1. **Executive summary** — answer the research question concisely and state the most important caveat.
2. **Research question and scope** — define the topic, geography, time horizon, audience, and exclusions.
3. **Key findings** — group findings by theme and attach source references to factual claims whenever sources are available.
4. **Technology and market signals** — distinguish observed signals from interpretation and forecasts.
5. **Opportunities** — describe who may benefit and under what conditions.
6. **Risks and constraints** — include technical, operational, financial, privacy, regulatory, and adoption risks where relevant.
7. **Evidence register** — title, publisher/author, publication date, URL or document location, claim supported, and evidence-quality note.
8. **Limitations and confidence** — identify missing data, stale sources, conflicting evidence, and assumptions.
9. **Recommendations and next steps** — list actions tied to findings and unresolved questions.

## Evidence rules

- Never fabricate a source, URL, quotation, statistic, publication date, or citation.
- Distinguish directly supported facts, analytical interpretations, hypotheses, and predictions.
- Prefer primary sources for technical specifications and official statistics where appropriate.
- Do not treat a source's presence in a source list as proof that its claims were verified.
- When no source evidence is available, say so and produce a research plan rather than a source-backed conclusion.
- If sources disagree, show the disagreement and explain what additional verification is needed.

## Evaluation checklist

- [ ] Does the summary answer the user's actual question?
- [ ] Can each important factual claim be traced to evidence?
- [ ] Are time-sensitive claims dated?
- [ ] Are assumptions and forecasts labelled?
- [ ] Are risks and uncertainty explicit?
- [ ] Can the user identify the next concrete action?
